Country grid assessment

Grid assessment indicators and country characteristics • trial data for five pilot countries

The state of the grid is assessed by country, based on the following definition of success: the grid is succeeding when it can accommodate changes on the supply and demand sides of the power system in a timely manner to provide clean, abundant, reliable and affordable electricity for all.

This is broken down into six elements - each is assessed in terms of its current state of constraint, the possible driver and level of future risk. A drop-down menu indicates where more than one indicator is available.

A summary at the top gives the headline constraint metric for each of the six elements assessed. This is followed by key country characteristics that provide important context for interpretation of the figures.

All sections are collapsible.

Prototype dataValues are calculated based on the data collected for the grid assessment data tool prototype, with the use of AI and with human review (version 11 Nov 2026). No thresholds or scores. Internal only: do not cite or circulate.

How the indicators are built from the workbook
  1. Every value is calculated from the trial data workbook. Nothing is invented or estimated in this page. Open "How this is calculated" under any chart for the formula and the workbook tabs used.
  2. Terminology follows the framework terminology document: six grid assessment indicators, each with a constraint metric, a possible driver and a future risk, plus six country characteristics.
  3. Ratios are calculated here, not in the workbook (for example curtailment GWh ÷ generation TWh). Where the workbook already holds the ratio (congestion cost per MWh, energy not supplied per TWh, peak-to-demand growth ratio), the stored value is shown.
  4. Money is in nominal local currency (Poland's plan investment is in 2025 prices). Levels are not comparable across countries; trends within a country are.
  5. Grid investment for South Korea and Pakistan is not national transmission: South Korea is KEPCO total capex (includes distribution and non-grid spending) and Pakistan is K-Electric (Karachi) only. Indicators using it are shown with that caveat in the calculation note.
  6. Plans vs outturn: renewables compare plan capacity with installed capacity (levels). Demand compares growth, indexed to each plan's first year, because plan demand definitions differ from Ember's.
  7. Projections are dashed, on a shaded band, with hollow markers. Where a metric compares pathways or plan editions, each has its own line.
  8. Gaps are shown, not hidden. Where the workbook has no data the box reads "Data not reported". An indicator with a single data point is shown as a single column.
  9. The summary tiles show the first constraint metric with data, its latest value and the relative change from the previous data point. No good or bad colouring: no direction or threshold has been agreed.
